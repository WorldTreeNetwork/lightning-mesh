Re-advise after the Fable r2 send-back amendment. The blind pass below was
written from the Why, the living `mesh-mini-apps` spec, and `contract.ts`
`appPath` before design, tasks, delta, or the prior reviews were opened.

## Blind pass — ten-line independent take

1. Pin the publish boundary to recognize app metadata only when the control TXT contains `app`.
2. Pin supported control metadata to exactly `app=v1`; an unsupported present marker must be rejected.
3. Pin absent `app` to ordinary-service behavior, leaving `path` and every other TXT value opaque.
4. Refuse any validator that interprets or rejects `path` when `app` is absent.
5. Pin marked records with no `path` to the consumer contract's `/` default.
6. Pin marked records with `path` to the exact `appPath` exclusions: non-leading slash, `//`, backslash, controls, or a scheme segment.
7. Pin both self-serve name claims and operator control publication to one shared validator and one answer.
8. Pin invalid marked publication to fail before spool or lease mutation, preserving the previous record.
9. Refuse treating publish-time validation as sufficient; directory and browser consumers must continue re-validation.
10. Tradeoff: keeping `path` opaque without `app` preserves generic TXT extensibility at the cost of allowing inert path-like garbage.

## Comparison and steelman

The amended change answers every concern in the blind take. The Shape section
now defines the control marker as present if and only if TXT `app` is present,
requires byte-exact `app=v1`, validates `path` only under that marker, and
explicitly preserves plain `publish --txt path=print`. Validation repeats the
same gate, retains the `/` default and the exact `contract.ts` `appPath`
exclusions, and keeps consumer re-validation. Rejection remains before spool or
lease mutation, and both publication surfaces still converge on one shared
validator.

The delta makes the invariant normative: absent `app` leaves `path` and every
other TXT key opaque and untouched. Its dedicated plain-service scenario proves
the acceptance's true branch, including successful publication and no mini-app
classification. The `publish_service` task now carries the same condition, so
an implementer following either design, requirement, scenario, or task cannot
reasonably apply app-path validation to an unmarked service.

Steelman against the blind take: allowing an arbitrary `path` on an unmarked
record can look inconsistent beside strict marked-path validation. It is not a
gap in this contract. `path` acquires mini-app semantics only through
`app=v1`; before that marker it is generic service TXT, and preserving it is
both backward-compatible and aligned with the consumer's `isMiniApp` gate.
The amendment therefore resolves the r2 send-back without broadening the app
contract or weakening validation of marked records.
