Steelman on take 6. The spec bounds what `no_blanket_route` can mean without
defining a mechanism. Clients SHALL NOT gain a route to every `10.42/16`
address, so it cannot be a blanket allow. The committed policy SHALL NOT
close reach to a published name, so it cannot be a blanket deny. Any adapter
slice that gives the token a mechanism is held between those two
requirements. Today no real adapter implements `SchedulesClients`, so the
token cannot be misread live. That is enough for this slice.

Steelman on take 7. In the spec, "the limit" refers to the no-starve rule in
the sentence just before it. That rule is stated in both the spec and
design.md, and the scheduler is explicitly not owed. Leaving a floor value
out of `Schedule` is consistent with "names the limit and does not choose the
scheduler". A numeric floor would be the cap the change refuses.

All r8 owed items (R8-1, R8-2, R8-3) are fixed in the spec, design, and code.
The anchor invariant holds. The committed policy text agrees with the reach
rule: `10.42.0.0/16` is not closed and is recorded as `no_blanket_route`, and
the management plane is closed.

## Notes (not owed)

- Spec wording: "The plan names the limit" could read as "The plan states the
  no-starve rule". That wording keeps a reader from expecting a field.
- Rename or drop `ClientClass::PublishedService`. Today it is an unreachable
  client class, while the spec says a published service is not one.
- `Destination::HouseRoute` doc (`client_class.rs:58`): drop "or the overlay".
- Carried from r8 and still true: `render_policy` takes only the SSID, so
  publishing a name does not move `proposed_revision`. The exceptions slice
  owes that. `required_health` is empty, which is safe only while no real
  adapter implements `SchedulesClients` (`2mgv`). A member mark on an open
  SSID can be spoofed, so `ai0.1` should keep it to schedule order and never
  give it reach.
