## Independent take (written before opening design.md or tasks.md)

1. Pin the existing `app_ula` derivation: raw Ed25519 key bytes, subnet 1, zero IID clamped to one; do not duplicate its identity rules.
2. Pin one owned Linux TUN with only the local app `/128`; computing an address is distinct from making it usable.
3. Require an explicit peer-address-to-32-byte-node-id-to-connection association; `spawn_encap_pair` itself neither selects nor authenticates a peer.
4. Require a concrete packet path in both directions, including any exact peer host route; local `/128` assignment alone does not route a remote echo.
5. Preserve TUN packet boundaries and choose an MTU compatible with the selected datagram transport; byte-stream mock success is limited evidence.
6. Reuse encap rather than the IPv4 `/31` and Babel link-local policy embedded in `spawn_tunnel`.
7. Own both task handles and the interface lifetime, including partial setup failure, disconnect, shutdown, and identity or guild changes.
8. Refuse covering routes, foreign-house forwarding, ULA Iroh candidates, and incidental Babel or client-SSID writes in this node.
9. Show the computed address in the existing guild surface without implying connectivity; absent inputs or denied TUN privileges must leave no partial network state.
10. Tradeoff: a real-TUN echo over an injected `DatagramConn` can validate this narrow seam cheaply, but cannot establish authenticated Iroh connectivity or two-Admin reachability.

## Comparison and steelman

The author answered the derivation, subnet, Linux scope, no covering prefix, existing UI, and separation from Iroh candidates. The proposal's journey deliberately narrows the Why to a second keyed process on the same host. That is a defensible increment: a supplied `DatagramConn` need not establish QUIC to exercise the app TUN. I do not require a QUIC handshake, foreign `/48` routing, or candidate binding for acceptance. The cited finding expressly claims only the seam and does not unlock either deferred node.

The remaining concerns are the boundaries an implementer would otherwise have to invent. They are not a demand for a different transport design.

## Owed before acceptance

1. **Pin the Admin inputs and the executable peer path.** `design.md` says “the house guild” and “selected by the peer's 32-byte node id,” but names neither the selection rule nor the connection provider. The existing ceremony creates both Admin and workplace house guilds (`admin/src-tauri/src/guild_ceremony.rs:234`); the guild panel has a changing selection (`admin/src/lib/guild/mock/GuildPanel.svelte`). `spawn_encap_pair` takes an already-selected connection and has no node-id argument or lookup. Admin currently depends on the mesh library without its optional `daemon` feature, and its startup has no transport owner (`admin/src-tauri/Cargo.toml`, `admin/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:38`). Specify the source of the one active house, actor key, peer address and node id; how the scoped second process supplies its connection; and where Admin starts that session. Explicit dependency injection is sufficient if the journey names its caller and runner. Pin exact peer `/128` routing and return routing, or another explicit injection path, without covering prefixes. The spike installs both peer host routes (`docs/spikes/admin-tun-encap/src/main.rs:84`), a prerequisite missing from the contract. Add a missing/unknown-peer case that cannot silently choose another connection. Keep a supplied peer label distinct from authenticated peer identity.

2. **Pin ownership, failure, and observable status.** The contract covers missing inputs and failure to open the TUN, but stops before address or route setup failure and running-session teardown. Define one owner for the device, both encap tasks, and any owned host routes; rollback after partial setup; shutdown/disconnect behavior; and guild/key change or removal behavior (a clearly bounded restart policy is acceptable). `EncapHandles` has explicit `abort`, not drop cleanup; the existing `Tunnel` supplies its own `Drop` to abort both tasks (`crates/mjolnir-mesh/src/tun/iface.rs`). Reusing only encap does not inherit that ownership. Specify the Linux privilege prerequisite/failure behavior and the non-Linux result. Carry the proposal's “computed but not up” distinction into the delta and verification, including address usability and a packet MTU consistent with the chosen seam. These are setup/teardown obligations, not a request to implement privilege elevation or a new control.

3. **Make the packet evidence discriminate seam delivery from local delivery.** The finding reports a real-TUN echo and correctly disclaims QUIC, but the checked-in reproducer assigns both destination addresses in the same network namespace, runs an unbound `ping` to one of those local addresses, and asserts only ping success (`docs/spikes/admin-tun-encap/src/main.rs:80–89,103–122`). Its in-memory `LabeledConn` does not assert a send/receive, and `peer` is only checked before the ping. From that source, local kernel delivery is a plausible bypass; the recorded success alone does not distinguish it from traversal of both encap loops. Reconcile this evidence before relying on `Unverified hypotheses: None`: require observed echo request/reply bytes on the intended connection and a disconnected/disabled-seam negative control that fails. Isolated network namespaces are one option, not a mandated design. Use derived fixture addresses in the integration acceptance. This remains a real-TUN/DatagramConn test, with no demand for an Iroh handshake or ULA candidate.
