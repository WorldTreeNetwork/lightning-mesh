## Blind take (written before design.md / tasks.md / steer.md / spec delta)

From Why, living specs, and cited code only:

1. Pin: Earth enters the mesh only through the gossip/CRDT book, projected into `directory.json` as an additive `Option` field (the `name` pattern at meshd.rs:2860). `radio.json` v1 stays RF-only, no lat/lon, no version bump.
2. Pin: absence is `None`/omitted, never `(0,0)`; the directory pattern gives that for free and the viewer must draw "unknown", not the Gulf of Guinea.
3. Pin: the router is not the surveyor. Routers have no GPS; a coordinate is a claim written by a handheld (the compass) about a node, carrying provenance (who stamped, when, how) the same way node-name is an operator-set book entry.
4. Pin: live store vs ball. The CRDT/directory is the live truth; a DreamBall is a signed snapshot derived from it for the viewer. A ball is never read back into the directory.
5. Pin: `/mesh` in web3d-space is the visualizer; hello.mesh TopologyPanel is unchanged; the viewer consumes a ball (or directory export), not the live CRDT.
6. Refuse: GPS or NFC silicon on OpenWrt nodes, and any coordinate field in `radio.json`.
7. Refuse: coverage or coordinates as identity or guild membership; a position is not key material.
8. Refuse: a fourth DreamBall axis. Coverage rides inside an existing axis (look, as an asset/attribute), not a PROTOCOL.md change.
9. Refuse: the compass growing lidar/camera in this capability; world capture is a separate device role with its own change.
10. Tradeoff: last-known coordinates in a CRDT go stale forever when a router moves. Accept LWW with timestamp + surveyor id and let the viewer age them into "thin/unknown", rather than building freshness or tombstones into gossip. Open concern the author must answer: publishing WGS84 of home routers into gossip and a shareable signed ball is a privacy call (precision, who exports), not an implementation detail.

## Why send-back: call 3 does not have a counterparty

Call 3 as written: "tap-range presence: BLE or ESP-NOW already on the
compass … OpenWrt nodes do not gain NFC silicon." Verified against the
cited trees:

- `northstar/.../features/network/ble_beacon.cpp:6` — BLE is
  **STATUS: DISABLED** (NimBLE NVS ops crash against the RGB LCD DMA).
  "Already on the compass" is true for ESP-NOW (`espnow_mesh.cpp`), not BLE.
- ESP-NOW is ESP32-to-ESP32 vendor action frames. The mt76 fleet has no
  ESP-NOW peer and no Bluetooth (`fleet-nodes.conf`, no `hci0` anywhere in
  `deploy/` or `docs/`). So a BLE/ESP-NOW "tap" has nothing to tap on a
  router. The only way to make call 3 true as written is to strap an
  ESP32 beacon to every router, which is the NFC-on-routers class of
  thing this change exists to refuse.
- The signal that does exist: the compass already scans Wi-Fi and reads
  per-BSSID RSSI (`apps/wifi/app.cpp:397,413`), and once associated to a
  node's client SSID it sits on that node's routed `/24` with the gateway
  at `10.42.<x>.1`. "Which node am I next to" is therefore the associated
  BSSID at a tap-range RSSI, and the counterparty is hello on the LAN
  gateway. BLE/ESP-NOW remain fine for compass-to-compass or
  compass-to-AI-Camera, but not for compass-to-router.

Writing call 3 into `ARCHITECTURE.md` as-is sends `add-compass-node-mark`
to build a stamp against a radio the router does not have. Fix is a few
lines: **proximity to a router is Wi-Fi association plus tap-range RSSI on
that node's client BSSID; BLE/ESP-NOW is peer-to-handheld only; routers
gain no NFC, BLE, or ESP-NOW silicon.**

## Second required change: pin the stamp ingress

design.md says "hello reads the projection, it does not become the
writer." That is already not the shape of the tree: hello has
`POST /api/identity` and `POST /api/name-claim`
(`crates/mjolnir-hello/src/routes.rs:428,504`), both signed-by-client,
verified-by-hello, spooled to disk, and swept by meshd (`--spool-dir`,
`mjolnir-meshd.rs:253`). The control API is loopback only
(`127.0.0.1:5380`, `mjolnir-meshd.rs:4703`), so a handheld on the client
LAN cannot reach it. Left as written, `add-node-coordinates` must choose
between exposing the control API, a new listener, or the spool, and that is
a fight the ADR should close now. Recommended pin: **a coordinate stamp is
a signed claim delivered to hello on the node's LAN gateway and spooled to
meshd, the same path as name-claim; hello holds no key and verifies only;
the CRDT book is the authority.** Replace the "does not become the writer"
sentence with "hello is a verifying spool, never the authority."

## Notes (not blocking)

- Join key. The delta says joinable by `node_id`, `mesh_mac`, or
  `backhaul_addr`. Today `radio.json` has `mesh_mac` + `backhaul_addr` and no
  `node_id` (`radio.rs:36-49`); `directory.json` has `node_id` +
  `backhaul_addr` and no `mesh_mac` (`mjolnir-meshd.rs:2860-2889`). The only
  key common to both documents is `backhaul_addr`. Either pin that as the
  join, or make `add-node-coordinates` owe `mesh_mac` on the directory
  entry. web3d-space's `MeshMember` already carries all three, which is why
  the fixture hides this.
- Privacy is a sixth call nobody made. Gossiping WGS84 of home routers
  means every node holds every node's position, and a signed ball is a
  shareable artifact of that. Decide precision (a coarse cell versus raw
  fix) and that ball export is an explicit operator act, or say why not.
  This belongs in the ADR, not in `add-dreamball-coverage`.
- Staleness (take line 10) is unaddressed. Last-known plus stamper plus
  time is LWW; say so, and say the viewer ages it, so nobody builds
  tombstones into gossip.
- `tasks.md` first checkbox is unchecked though proposal, design, steer and
  deltas exist.
- Delta scenario "Radio snapshot stays RF" keys on "WHEN this capability is
  folded"; fine as a citation target, odd as a scenario. Leave it.
