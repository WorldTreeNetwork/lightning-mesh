## Comparison with the blind take

**R1 is answered.** `known_house_router` (`mjolnir-meshd.rs:8574`) observes a usable address, so it cannot establish the configured-house exclusion. The revised design instead derives that exclusion from the valid guild file through `house_ula_prefix`, explicitly covering unusable addresses, absent or malformed guild input, and a guild change. The delta includes the configured-house/unusable-address contrast. Retaining a last successful projection does not waive the rule to exclude the currently configured house on each reconcile.

**R2 is answered.** The reader accepts only canonical `fd00::/8` `/48` network addresses, skips the compiled prefix obtained from `ula_prefix()` (`tun/link.rs:44`), and does not pretend to authenticate arbitrary prefixes. The direct-renderer `fd12:3456:789a::/48` scenario explicitly emits an allow. Its absence in the happy-path fixture therefore demonstrates publisher selection, not a hidden stock-prefix denylist. The living stock-prefix protection remains a publisher responsibility at this seam.

**R3 is answered.** Both current loops can lose an unsuccessful application: LAN retries depend on the write's `changed` result (`mjolnir-meshd.rs:8016`), while overlay also advances `last_rendered` after a failed service call (`mjolnir-meshd.rs:8629`). The revised contract requires pending application until start/restart succeeds, retries with identical inputs and disk contents, and retains paced execution. The failed-removal scenario exercises the consequential case. Missing input clears; other read errors preserve the last successful projection; an initial unreadable projection must not manufacture a removal.

The remaining blind concerns are covered: both renders gain only ordered `eq 128` in/out allows; local export remains usability-gated; foreign addresses and covering prefixes are not assigned or redistributed; duplicate/order handling is deterministic; gateway behavior, Iroh's IPv4 bind, and the existing exact-host demux remain intact. The delta retains the existing requirement and scenarios while adding the admission contract.

## Steelman and tradeoff

My strongest objection would be to retain no foreign permission after an unreadable file, or require the router to prove the file belongs to its current house. That would change this seam's authority and failure policy. The author explicitly places pair validation and house-transition replacement with a trusted publisher, while preserving routing availability during read errors. Prefix bytes cannot prove that relationship, and a stock-prefix blacklist would not supply the missing proof. This is a coherent bounded reader contract.

The cost is explicit: a stale projection can retain authorization, and an old-house line can count after a guild change until the publisher replaces the projection. There is no expiry or withdrawal op. Acceptance does not claim immediate revocation under I/O failure, nor delivery of the later publisher or end-to-end admission workflow.
