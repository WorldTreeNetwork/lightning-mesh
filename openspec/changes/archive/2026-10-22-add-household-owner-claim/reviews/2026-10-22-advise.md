# Independent architecture advise — add-household-owner-claim

> **ADVISE:** send-back
> **READER:** opus-arch-review
> **SPAWN:** .spawns/mjolnir-mesh-bf7.1-1792653881-83218-95513d16

## Blind pass (written before opening design.md / tasks.md / spec delta)

Sources: proposal Why, household-authority delta (Physical claim anchors authority;
Recovery does not create a takeover path; recovery-window timing), and
`deploy/openwrt/files/etc/rc.wps/00-mjolnir-wan-admin` (the button is already
consumed for the WAN-SSH toggle).

1. **Pin where the secret lives.** If the one-time secret is on the device label, a
   neighbor with hands on the box holds secret + button; both factors are physical.
   Pin: secret ships off-device (card/packaging), node stores only a verifier, never
   shown on any setup screen, consumed on success, attempt-limited.
2. **Pin channel protection.** hello.mesh serves plaintext :80 on an open client AP.
   The secret must not cross that wire in a reusable form (PAKE / proof bound to the
   claim nonce + owner pubkey), or a sniffing neighbor replays it in the same window.
3. **Pin window↔claim binding.** The press must confirm one specific pending claim
   (node id, epoch, owner pubkey digest, nonce), not arm a global flag that the first
   concurrent request consumes. First durable CAS wins; no overwrite on retry.
4. **Pin button routing.** One button, now two meanings (WAN-SSH toggle vs ceremony
   presence). Pin the rc.wps ordering/state machine; a WAN-SSH press must never
   count as ownership presence, and a ceremony press must not open WAN SSH.
5. **Pin timing.** Window on monotonic clock, closed on reboot/consumption; no wall
   clock. Pin the first-claim window length (living spec only pins recovery 120 s).
6. **Pin recovery effects.** Recovery ⇒ epoch++, all prior recovery creds retired,
   outstanding grants dead at the new epoch, per-node ack; old owner key not usable.
7. **Pin fork semantics.** Two partitioned recoveries from the same parent epoch:
   no deterministic tie-break promotes one; privileged ops fail closed, ordinary
   connectivity unaffected, and the resolution path (one more ceremony?) is named.
8. **Refuse:** button-only claim; secret derivable from node_id/MAC/serial; WPS
   window as presence; hello holding owner private keys or bearer grants; a reset
   that keeps the former house's secrets.
9. **Refuse:** claim state in gossip/CRDT as the source of truth for "unclaimed"
   (first-gossip-wins); the node's local durable record is authoritative.
10. **Tradeoff the author must answer:** lost secret before first claim. Off-device
    secret is stronger against the neighbor but losable → node unclaimable without a
    vendor/RMA path; on-label is recoverable but collapses to physical-only. Which,
    and what does a destructive reset do to the secret?

## Compare (after opening design.md, tasks.md, spec delta)

Scope: contract and design acceptance only, at HEAD `bbb3040`. Nothing here
claims built behavior. Settled decisions are not reopened: recovery needs
enrolled credential plus physical presence, the 120 s window, the profile
lifetimes, no TPM or factory attestation, and no custom key exchange.

What the design gets right, and what matches the blind take:

- **Timing (blind 5).** The window is 120 s, consumed by one success, and
  closes on reboot or loss of trustworthy timing. The design also separates it
  cleanly from grant lifetimes ("Clocks"). Matches.
- **Atomic single winner (blind 3, 9).** The design says "persisted atomically
  against the node id … unclaimed to 1; a racing second claim cannot overwrite
  it". It keeps the local durable record authoritative and does not use
  gossip. Matches.
- **Recovery retires credentials (blind 6).** Success or transfer retires every
  prior recovery credential, and enrollment is epoch-scoped. Mostly matches;
  see N2.
- **Fork (blind 7).** It persists candidate heads and never picks a winner. A
  successor needs a new ceremony naming one parent, and the change delegates
  verification to `add-control-record-integrity`. Matches, and the hand-off is
  clean.
- **Refusals (blind 8).** WPS protocol, developer SSH and discovery do not
  count as ownership. Button-only and secret-only claims fail. Matches.

Where the design falls short of its own Why ("a neighbor who can press a button
**or see a setup screen** must not be able to take that role"):

### R1 — The secret must not be shown on the node's own setup screen (blocking)

design.md §Ceremony 2 says the secret is "shown once to the purchaser, on a
printed card **or on that node's own first-claim screen**." These routers have
no display, so the "first-claim screen" is the hello.mesh page. That page is
served on the client AP, which `setup-wireless.sh:45` defaults to **open**
(`CLIENT_ENC=none`). The secret is supposed to be the one factor that does not
come from being physically near the device. If the node shows it, it becomes
another proximity factor. "Shown once" means shown to the first visitor, and
that visitor can be the neighbor. Then the neighbor needs only the button, and
the claim collapses to the button-only case the Why rules out.

Steelman: the Why says "button **or** screen", so a neighbor who has only one of
them fails. That works for the card. It fails for the screen, because anyone
who can reach the button can also join the open AP.

**Required pin:** the secret ships off-device only (card or packaging). The
node never displays it, before or after a reset. Also state what a destructive
reset does to the stored hash and to the node id. That is the blind-10
tradeoff: does a reset re-arm the original card, so the card goes with the
device on resale and a thief holding the card can claim the node; or is the
node unclaimable without a re-provision path? Pick one and say it.

### R2 — The raw secret must not cross plaintext HTTP (blocking)

design.md §Keys says hello handlers never receive the secret **"after the
comparison"**. So the raw secret is posted to hello, and the spec delta
"hub does not receive owner secrets" repeats the "after the hash comparison"
wording. hello is `tiny_http::Server::http`
(`crates/mjolnir-hello/src/main.rs:43`) on an open AP. One-time use and window
consumption do **not** stop the active case. A rogue AP using the same open
SSID, or ARP spoofing on the LAN, relays the purchaser's secret with the
attacker's owner public key. The purchaser has already pressed the button, so
the window is open, and the attacker's request is the first complete claim.
The device signature over the owner key lets the purchaser *detect* this only
after the binding exists. Recovery cannot undo it, because the attacker
enrolls the only recovery credential.

This conflicts with the stated constraint against custom key exchange only if
the fix is a hand-rolled PAKE. It does not have to be.

**Required pin (one of):**
- (a) The claim carries a MAC keyed from the secret over the owner public key,
  node id, epoch and ceremony nonce. Use standard HMAC or HKDF; the raw secret
  never leaves the client. Pin an entropy floor high enough that a sniffed MAC
  cannot be ground offline (≥ ~80 bits, which is roughly 16 base32 characters
  on the card).
- (b) Run the claim only over a channel authenticated to a node-id fingerprint
  printed on the card.

Either way, change the delta wording to "never receives the raw provisioning
secret". Also pin a per-window attempt cap and say how the stored hash is
salted or stretched.

### R3 — Name who verifies device proof and against what (blocking)

§Ceremony 1 lists "the node's meshd identity key signs the request" as an
acceptance factor. But the node is also the verifier, so the node signing its
own input is not a factor against the neighbor. Any request that reaches the
node gets that signature. Its real job is the other direction: it shows the
**purchaser's client** that it bound to the intended device and not to a
hostile entry router. That is the job the living spec's "verified
device-specific proof" points at.

The scenario "GIVEN the provisioning secret and a valid device signature"
treats the signature as a claimant input, which makes this worse.

**Required pin:** the client (Lightning Admin or hub JS) verifies the node
signature against an expected node id. The node id comes from the card, which
couples to R1. Do not show success, or send the R2 proof, until that check
passes. Restate the three factors honestly: two gate the node (secret and
button) and one gates the client (device proof).

### R4 — Dispatch the button against the existing WAN-SSH hook (blocking)

The design reuses "the same button position as WPS". Today
`deploy/openwrt/files/etc/rc.wps/00-mjolnir-wan-admin` takes that press, runs
`mjolnir-wan-admin toggle` and exits 0, which stops the rc.wps chain. The
proposal lists the WAN-SSH window as out of scope ("presence only; not
ownership"), but the design never says how one press is routed. As written,
three things are possible:
- a claim press also opens WAN↔LAN SSH, which is a security side effect on a
  brand-new node;
- a press meant for WAN-SSH arms an ownership or recovery window;
- the claim hook never runs, because rc.wps already broke out of the chain.

**Required pin:** a dispatch rule. For example, on an unclaimed node or one
with a pending recovery request, the press arms the ceremony only and does not
toggle WAN; otherwise it toggles WAN. Or use a long press against a short
press. Name the process that owns window state: meshd holds the window and
the CAS, the rc.wps hook only signals meshd, and hello only relays.

### Non-blocking (answer in the design or a task)

- **N1 Process boundary.** State that meshd does the hash compare, owns the
  window and runs the durable CAS, and that hello only relays. Today hello
  feeds meshd through an asynchronous spool, so name the claim's request and
  response path and its timeout.
- **N2 Epoch effects of recovery.** Say explicitly that recovery advances the
  ownership epoch **and** the authority epoch, so the old owner's outstanding
  grants die under the household-authority rotation and per-node ack rules.
  The proposal names both epochs; the design only implies it.
- **N3 Node behavior during a fork.** On a node holding one candidate head,
  are privileged operations refused (fail closed) until a successor exists? Say
  that ordinary connectivity is unaffected.
- **N4 Order of window and request.** Arm-then-consume is acceptable once R2
  binds the owner public key into the secret proof. Otherwise prefer
  request-then-press, as the living spec says ("physical confirmation bound to
  the request").
- **N5 Existing fleet.** Nodes installed by developers have no card. Name the
  provisioning or migration path, or say they stay developer-owned until
  re-provisioned.
- **N6 Tests.** Add: sniffed or relayed secret with a substituted owner key;
  WAN-SSH press does not arm the ceremony and a ceremony press does not open
  WAN; reboot closes the window; attempt cap; reset behavior from R1; a fork
  node refuses privileged operations.

## Verdict

**Send-back.** The skeleton is right: an atomic single winner, separate
clocks, credential retirement on recovery, and a fork that never picks a
winner. But two pins contradict the change's own Why on the shipped network
(an open AP and plaintext hello). One is the secret shown on the setup
screen (R1). The other is the raw secret sent to hello before comparison
(R2). Device proof (R3) and button dispatch (R4) are underspecified in ways
an implementer would have to invent. All four are wording and design fixes;
none of them reopens a settled decision.
