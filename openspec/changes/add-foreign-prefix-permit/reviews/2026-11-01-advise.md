## Independent take — written before opening design.md or tasks.md

1. Pin reciprocal, independently verified consent: a single signature must produce no admission or route side effect.
2. Pin each signature to both raw guild identities, the intended direction, and the exact permitted prefix so unrelated approvals cannot be paired.
3. Pin signer authority to an authenticated house roster; a valid Ed25519 signature alone does not establish permission to speak for a house.
4. Pin prefix validation to `house_ula_prefix`: the 20 literal domain bytes plus raw 32-byte guild id, with exactly a derived `/48`.
5. Refuse arbitrary stock ULA, broader masks, noncanonical host bits, self-pairs, and ambiguous collisions between distinct house identities.
6. Refuse unsigned or malformed evidence and define deterministic duplicate, conflicting, expired, or withdrawn-consent handling before consumers depend on it.
7. Preserve the `keyspace.*` wire vocabulary and refuse `guild.*`; extending the current four-kind contract requires an explicit spec delta.
8. Add deliberate handling in `guild_view::apply_op` so a permit cannot break guild listing or silently change membership.
9. Keep permit evidence separate from route installation, radio writes, and reachability claims; retain existing ULA and Babel boundaries for this node.
10. Tradeoff: a narrow initial admission model can defer revocation and distributed roster machinery only if its trust source, lifetime, and downstream limitations are explicit.

## Comparison with the contract

The record and predicate in [design.md](../design.md), the [foreign-prefix-permit delta](../specs/foreign-prefix-permit/spec.md), and the [admin-guild delta](../specs/admin-guild/spec.md) answer points 1, 2's pair binding, 4, 7, 8, and 9. Six prefix bytes avoid mask and host-bit ambiguity. Reciprocal guild ids reject unrelated pairings and self-pairs. The admin delta preserves the earlier join/assign/nest/no-rename requirements and explicitly protects the projection. Reusing `house_ula_prefix` preserves the existing byte contract and vectors.

Two contract gaps remain before implementation:

1. **Blocking: signature validity does not establish house consent.** `design.md:17` and `specs/foreign-prefix-permit/spec.md:5` require only signature verification, distinct guild ids, and a derived prefix. An unrelated actor X can sign `(keyspace=A, peer=B, prefix=house_ula_prefix(B))` and `(keyspace=B, peer=A, prefix=house_ula_prefix(A))`, with matching targets. Both pass every stated check even though neither house consented. Requiring two different actors would not fix this. Pin the authority source and eligibility rule binding `op.actor` to each admitting house, when that authority is evaluated, and refusal when authority is absent or unverifiable. A caller-supplied trusted authority snapshot can be sufficient; distributed log transport need not enter this node. Do not substitute the current display projection for authenticated authority: `admin/src-tauri/src/guild_view.rs:236` folds joins and changes, including replacement rosters, without authorizing the actor; `admin/src-tauri/src/guild.rs:108` signs caller-supplied guild ids. Neither supplies the missing check.
2. **Blocking: target/body consistency is not part of validation.** `design.md:5` says the target is the admitting guild id, but the normative direction predicate never requires a present `target_fp` equal to body `keyspace`. A correctly signed op targeting C while its body admits on behalf of A satisfies the stated predicate. Pin rejection of missing or mismatched targets at the verifying boundary, alongside the existing raw-byte field lengths and reciprocal-body checks. Test this with an otherwise authorized signer so the test exercises target binding rather than failing on authority first.

## Steelman against the independent take

This is a small evidence predicate, not a federation system. A local trusted operator can create both records without remote synchronization, and downstream nodes own route installation. That supports deferring log copying, routing, revocation machinery, and a general membership redesign. It does not make arbitrary signed records proof of consent: if trust is supplied by the caller, that precondition and the actor-to-house binding must be explicit in the contract consumed by those downstream nodes.

I also withdraw collision rejection as a required expansion of this node: the living identity-derived-ula spec explicitly leaves collisions undetected. The record retains both full guild ids, so later route consumers must not treat a `/48` alone as a unique house identity. Expiry and withdrawal are likewise not blockers for this bounded first version; this contract provides no revocation or expiry, and consumers cannot claim those guarantees. Duplicate valid evidence need not become a new state machine for a pure pair predicate.

The skipped spike need not be reinstated to answer these contract questions. The `Unverified hypotheses` section says none; a spike remains optional, not a prerequisite or substitute for pinning authorization.
