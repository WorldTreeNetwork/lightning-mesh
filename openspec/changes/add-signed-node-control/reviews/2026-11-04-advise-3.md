## Independent take — written before reading design.md or tasks.md

1. Pin authorization at the destination; forwarding and transport authentication cannot grant execution rights.
2. Preserve the canonical request and exact raw token bytes through both adapters, with bounded decoding before authorization.
3. Reserve the complete ReplayObligation atomically and durably before giving an executor any verified operation.
4. Refuse missing, unreadable, corrupt, or unpersistable ledger state; never initialize it while serving a request.
5. Preserve consumed reservations and the monotone time floor across cancellation, executor failure, and restart.
6. Pin one bounded, length-prefixed request and response per iroh stream, including completion and timeout behavior.
7. Keep unsigned transport/context errors distinguishable from signed destination receipts and from outcome 1.
8. Accept receipts only against the caller's expected destination key, complete obligation, and operation code.
9. Refuse substituted receipts, unknown outcomes, non-canonical encodings, and trailing bytes even when a signature verifies.
10. Tradeoff: durable at-most-once execution sacrifices retry availability after an uncertain effect; refusal cannot prove no effect.

## Comparison and steelman

Both gaps from review 2 are closed in the amended contract.

- **Framing and errors:** delta line 58 and design lines 39–43 pin one exchange per iroh stream, a big-endian u32 request length capped at 65536, the envelope, and a length-prefixed receipt or error response. The design ends the stream after the response and rejects bytes beyond the delimited body. Hello selects the node from the canonical request without authorizing it, forwards unchanged bytes, and stops at five seconds. Malformed input, missing context, timeout, and oversize use the closed `lightning-admin/control-error` array; these frames are unsigned, distinct from outcome-1 receipts. This no longer requires an entry without the signing key, or a malformed request without an obligation, to manufacture a receipt.
- **Receipt acceptance:** delta lines 58–63 and design line 37 explicitly require the expected destination key, all four obligation fields, and the expected operation code. The outer body is a CBOR byte string containing the canonical receipt array. Unknown outcomes, non-canonical bytes, and trailing data are refused. The corrected substitution scenario preserves signature validity while rejecting a different expected obligation or operation. Design line 43 and the delta explicitly deny that outcome 1 proves no effect occurred.
- **Earlier review bundles:** design lines 27–31 retain atomic exclusive reservation, explicit initialization, durable consumption across failure/cancellation/restart, and a persisted monotone floor. Design lines 47–53 and delta line 66 retain destination-owned context, local-node enforcement, opaque challenge semantics, and verified dispatch with plan digest/revision matching. The proposal correctly distinguishes an initialized empty ledger from a missing file.

The strongest objection from the blind take is that a refusal or interrupted exchange can conceal a completed effect. The amended contract answers it with permanently consumed reservations and explicitly uncertain outcomes; stronger transactional execution or a receipt cache is not necessary for this at-most-once slice. Likewise, the receipt intentionally carries the closed operation code rather than duplicating the signed arguments: correlation requires the expected complete obligation, while destination authorization and verified dispatch bind the arguments. These are acceptable scoped choices, not reasons to reopen the contract.
