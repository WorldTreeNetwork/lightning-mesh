## Comparison and steelman

The amended contract answers the independent concerns without enlarging this node. `design.md:9` makes the runner an explicit library entry called by a test or second process, with the peer /128, node id, and supplied connection kept together. The existing guild surface only displays the computed address. Requiring automatic desktop activation, a real QUIC handshake, or authenticated peer discovery would exceed this increment. A supplied connection label is not evidence of authenticated identity, and `up` is not evidence that a remote peer answered.

The strongest objection from the take is that owning task handles does not itself make cleanup happen. The amendment now addresses that objection directly: the session waits for either task, rather than assuming connection closure wakes the blocked TUN reader. Another possible objection is the lack of a separate guild/key-replacement scenario. The normative drop-on-change rule and identical drop cleanup already make that transition testable; another scenario heading is not needed to resolve this contract. Implementation verification must exercise it, along with idle disconnect and partial setup failure.

## The two r2 obligations

1. **House selection: answered.** `design.md:19` and `specs/admin-app-tun/spec.md:5` agree that the selected workplace house with no outer yields its app address, while a nested access guild yields none. The paired scenarios at delta lines 33 and 39 pin both outcomes and prohibit opening a TUN from display. This agrees with `save_genesis` in `admin/src-tauri/src/guild_ceremony.rs:220`, which records the workplace with profile `house`. The previous workplace exclusion is gone.
2. **Termination, ownership, and status: answered.** `design.md:11` explicitly makes the session observe either task returning and abort the sibling, delete its peer route, remove its address, and report the computed address with `up=false`. Delta line 7 requires the same behavior, including drop and caller-driven drop on guild/key change. This covers the asymmetry in `spawn_encap_pair`: receive closure can return while transmit remains blocked on the TUN. Startup `up` requires a usable /128 (neither tentative nor DAD-failed), an installed peer /128 route, and both tasks running. The route-failure and conn-close scenarios at delta lines 45 and 51 pin rollback and teardown; they do not require a further TUN read before cleanup.

The remaining boundaries are consistent: MTU 1280, exact host routes, no fallback connection, no Iroh candidate/bind changes, no Babel changes, and no client-SSID writes. Foreign /48 routing and overlay demultiplexing remain outside this node.
