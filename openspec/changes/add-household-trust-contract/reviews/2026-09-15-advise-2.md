## Blind pass (written before opening design.md / tasks.md / delta)

From the Why, living-specs, `portal.rs` and `secure-context-and-control-plane.md`
alone, I would pin: (1) owner ≠ issuer ≠ holder ≠ member as distinct keys; (2) every
consumer verifies, and the contract defines the *predicate*, not just record shape;
(3) authorization bound to exact target-node + op + full args + challenge + epoch +
request-id (defeats altered-target/holder + replay together); (4) monotonic authority
epoch surviving reboot, independent of wall-clock; (5) fail-closed + explicit
uncertainty when time/validity can't be established; (6) recovery = enrolled credential
+ physical presence, epoch-rotating, revocable; (7) physical proof bound to *this* node
+ fresh nonce; (8) refuse membership/label→admin, first-gossip-wins, entry-router
ownership, remote-only recovery; (9) refuse over-deferral to runtime such that two
downstream impls can't agree on canonical bytes. **Tradeoff the author must answer:**
safety vs availability under partition + uncertain time — fail-closed protects a house
with a hostile entry router but bricks legitimate offline ops; fail-open lets a
revoked/expired holder act. The contract must pick a default and surface uncertainty,
not choose silently.

## Verdict against the acceptance contrast

The contract **cleanly distinguishes** a valid narrower owner-authorized operation from
each attack the acceptance names, each anchored to a delta scenario:

- valid narrower op → "Narrower grant" (attenuate Wi-Fi mgmt → diagnostics on one node)
- membership / role label → "Changed holder or issuer" ("even if directory records
  label Bob an admin"); design Principals: IdentiKey/keyspace membership + Wi-Fi
  association confer nothing
- altered-holder / untrusted-issuer → same scenario; verifier facts not replaceable by
  token assertions; holder-change is authorized *reissuance*, never an attenuation caveat
- stale-authority → "Clock rollback" + trusted time floor + pending-enforcement model
- replay → "Lost reply and restart"; one-use challenge/request-id, durable serialize

All nine blind pins are answered. Two are answered **better** than my take: the design
rejects the cited doc's own suggestion that the WPS WAN-SSH window is physical proof
("WPS-like, not the WPS authentication protocol"; discovery/SSH are not proof), and it
resolves my open tradeoff exactly right — privileged ops fail closed with a
time/recovery-unavailable reason while *ordinary internet and hub use carry no admin
expiry* (consistent with `portal.rs` default-open). Stale authority is bounded by grant
expiry, revocation shows pending until per-node acknowledgement, and no UI may claim
global revocation while acknowledgements are missing. Untrusted gossip cannot roll back
authority. This is the honest partition posture.

Delta hygiene is compliant: all `ADDED`, every requirement has ≥1 `#### Scenario`,
capability id kebab-case, `ACTIVE BUILD` banner present. `tasks.md` intentionally omits
checkboxes and defers owed work to beads (ai0.3 + downstream); this satisfies the
project's beads policy but is a soft tension with living-specs' "checkboxes SHALL
represent work this change owes" — fold tooling must tolerate the beads-tracked form.

## Blocking findings (record in beads; resolve before dependent impl claims safety)

These are refinements/omissions, not structural contradictions — hence *accept*, not
send-back — but each is a contract-level invariant a downstream implementer could
otherwise miss.

1. **Recovery-credential lifecycle across transfer/recovery is unspecified — residual
   takeover path.** Recovery "must rotate authority epochs and reject previously issued
   *control grants*," and transfer requires existing owner authority. Neither states
   that prior **recovery credentials** are invalidated on ownership transfer or on a
   completed recovery. Absent that, a former owner (e.g. after a house sale) retains an
   enrolled recovery credential and, with physical access, satisfies both recovery
   factors against the new owner's house. The contract SHOULD assert: ownership transfer
   and recovery invalidate all previously enrolled recovery credentials (re-enrollment
   required), and enrollment is itself an epoch-scoped privileged operation. (Owed: bf7.1.)

2. **Cross-node authority convergence / anti-split-brain is thin.** The contract binds
   one initial owner via a genesis record and per-node ownership epoch, and correctly
   makes revocation/recovery per-node ("offline nodes remain outstanding"). But it does
   not assert a total, chained order on house authority transitions, so two isolated
   subsets each completing a local recovery could converge into competing owner epochs.
   The contract SHOULD state that authority transitions form a monotonic per-house
   sequence chained/signed from the prior authority (or recovery credential), and that
   nodes accept only forward, provenance-verified transitions. This is the one place I
   flag as a possible **architecture decision to confirm in design** (resolvable within
   bf7.1/ai0.9; not a new human approval gate).

3. **Canonical encoding needs a single normative source.** The argument digest and
   token/challenge binding require byte-for-byte agreement across issuer, holder and
   *three independent* downstream consumers (ai0.1 tokens, ai0.9 non-ingesting
   verifiers, b6j.2 transport). Deferring encodings to ai0.1 is correct scope, but the
   contract SHOULD name ai0.1's canonical encoding as the single artifact ai0.9/b6j.2
   consume, so they do not each invent one and silently diverge.

4. **Issuer-key revocation (mid-life, not via full owner recovery) is implied but not
   named.** "Verified current authority state" covers it, but the flow by which a
   destination learns an approved issuer pin is withdrawn — distinct from grant expiry —
   deserves an explicit constraint alongside epoch rotation. (Owed: ai0.9 / ai0.1.)

## Non-blocking notes

- **Time-floor bootstrap is a hard dependency, not a defect.** A node that never had
  trusted time refuses all absolute-expiry privileged ops until it obtains time
  evidence — the correct safe failure, but it means privileged offline operation depends
  on the time-evidence primitive owed to ai0.1 existing; worth stating as a dependency.
- **design.md structure:** an empty `## Qualification owed downstream` header precedes
  `## Delegated v1 timing policy` and a `(continued)` section. Cosmetic; tidy before fold
  so the ADR reads linearly.
