## Blind take (pre-registered, before opening design.md / tasks.md / delta)

Formed from proposal **Why/What/Impact**, `living-specs/spec.md`, the cited
control-plane doc (`docs/network-coordination/secure-context-and-control-plane.md`)
and `crates/mjolnir-hello/src/portal.rs` only. These are the concerns the author
must have answered — not a competing design. (A prior reader's note
`notes/2026-09-15-incomplete-reader.md` timed out before verdict; this take was
formed independently and converges with it — corroboration, not reliance.)

1. **PIN — verifier-only trust.** Every authorization decision is made by the
   destination from cryptographic evidence alone; the entry/forwarding router,
   browser, gossip and discovery record are carriers, never trust inputs. This is
   the whole point of "valid when the entry router / browser / discovery record is
   hostile." `portal.rs` confirms the public front desk establishes no ownership.
2. **PIN — four-way separation with teeth.** owner ≠ issuer ≠ holder ≠ member,
   defined so keyspace membership and a role label grant *zero* node-operation
   authority (the explicit refusal in Why). A grant names issuer, holder and
   target; altering any is a verification failure.
3. **PIN — full operation binding.** Each invocation binds (target-node-id,
   operation, complete args, challenge/nonce, authority epoch, request-id); this
   is what separates a narrower owner-authorized op from altered-holder/replay.
4. **PIN — reboot-survivable replay + monotonic epoch.** Nonce/epoch/sequence
   state persists across reboot and is monotonic; a node that lost replay/freshness
   state fails **closed** for privileged ops, with a defined recovery ceremony.
5. **PIN — uncertain-time posture.** A rebooted offline node cannot trust
   wall-clock; the contract must define behaviour when trusted time is unavailable
   and must never let a reset clock resurrect stale/revoked authority.
6. **PIN — physical proof for bootstrap + recoverable root distinct from device
   keys.** Ownership originates from physical presence, never first-gossip-claim;
   recovery uses a root separate from routine device keys and is not a claim bypass.
7. **REFUSE — no client-declared trust.** Never trust a client-asserted
   custody/role flag; never derive authority from reachability, gossip presence or
   an attractive label.
8. **REFUSE — no shared authority key/origin** (and no developer SSH key) that
   makes every router operator an implicit authority; issuer secrets never copied
   to every router.
9. **API-GAP I'd block on downstream.** A canonical byte-exact encoding of the
   signed request/argument digest as a *shared* invariant across signer and
   verifier, an explicit bound-field list, attenuation (narrow-only) semantics and
   negative vectors — else ai0.1 / b6j.2 / ai0.9 diverge.
10. **TRADEOFF / HUMAN DECISION.** Offline-validity window length vs revocation
    latency, and fail-closed-on-authority vs fail-open-on-public-browsing under
    partition/uncertain-time — the contract must pick a side and own the
    brick-vs-replay / availability-vs-safety cost; the *magnitude* is a policy call.

## Comparison — how the design answers the take (steelman)

The design directly answers 9 of 10 pins, several more precisely than I framed them.

- **Pin 1 (verifier-only) — answered.** "Trusted facts come from that verifier …
  A token-supplied fact cannot override ambient verifier facts or confer issuer
  trust"; delta "Verifier facts SHALL NOT be replaceable by token assertions";
  "Untrusted gossip cannot roll back authority"; HTTP adapter "may relay opaque
  requests but cannot supply trusted confirmation text."
- **Pin 2 (four-way separation) — answered, strongest part.** Principals section
  keeps owner/issuer/holder distinct even on one device; "An IdentiKey/keyspace
  membership record and Wi-Fi association confer none of them"; "Admin, Member and
  Guest are UI presets, not an ordered numeric privilege hierarchy." Delta scenario
  *Changed holder or issuer*: refuses "even if public directory records label Bob
  an admin." This is exactly the Why refusal, with a scenario.
- **Pin 3 (operation binding) — answered.** "binds protocol version, house, node,
  authority epoch, operation and all arguments to a one-use challenge/request
  identifier."
- **Pin 4 (replay/epoch) — largely answered.** Durable serialization against
  replay+revocation; retry-after-restart returns a recorded receipt or
  pending/unknown, not re-execution (delta *Lost reply and restart*). See Finding A
  for the one residual edge.
- **Pin 5 (uncertain time) — answered, matches my posture.** "Persist a trusted
  time floor and authority revision; clock rollback cannot extend a grant. If the
  target cannot establish validity after reboot, privileged operation is refused
  with a … time-unavailable reason rather than accepting indefinitely." Exact
  clock-evidence protocol + max offline validity deferred to ai0.1 with negative
  vectors gated. Delta *Clock rollback* scenario present.
- **Pin 6 (physical bootstrap + recovery) — answered, and more conservative than
  the control-plane doc.** The design explicitly declines to bless the existing
  WPS WAN-SSH window as sufficient proof and pushes channel/ceremony selection to
  bf7.1; recovery requires owner-rooted or pre-enrolled recovery credential, rotates
  authority epochs and rejects prior grants; a button on an owned node cannot
  replace the owner (delta *Button on an owned node*).
- **Pins 7–8 (refusals) — answered.** Client-declared trust rejected; role/label
  rejected; "Issuer private keys must not be copied to every router. Destinations
  need public issuer pins … not minting secrets."
- **Pin 9 (attenuation) — answered well.** "Attenuation may intersect operations,
  resources and time, never widen them. A change of holder is not accomplished by
  adding a contradictory holder caveat." Delta *Delegation preserves ceilings*
  requires holder change to be authorized bounded reissuance preserving parent
  ceiling/lifetime/revocation lineage — this closes the holder-swap-via-caveat
  attack cleanly. Canonical encodings/vectors honestly deferred to ai0.1.

**Acceptance contrast (packet `if_true`) — met on all five distinctions:**

| Distinction | Where it is enforced |
|---|---|
| Narrower owner-authorized op vs membership | Delta *Destination enforces bounded agency* + *Narrower grant*; "membership SHALL NOT grant administration" |
| Altered holder | Delta *Changed holder or issuer* + *Delegation preserves ceilings* |
| Untrusted issuer | *Changed holder or issuer* (unapproved issuer refused); "approved issuer" pin |
| Stale authority | *Durable freshness and honest revocation* + *Clock rollback* + epoch rotation on recovery |
| Replay | *Lost reply and restart* + one-use challenge + durable serialization |

Implementation boundaries are honest throughout ("not a claim those acceptance
tests already pass"; runtimes and negative vectors named to bf7.1/ai0.1/ai0.9/b6j.2).
This squarely satisfies `if_true`. The findings below are downstream refinements to
record, **not** contradictions that prevent using the contract (`if_false` not triggered).

## Findings for the conductor to record in beads (blocking downstream, not the contract)

Per project beads policy these are recorded here, not as task checkboxes.

- **F-A — fail-closed on lost/unavailable freshness ledger (severity: medium; target b6j.2, verify ai0.9).**
  The contract mandates durable serialization and refusal when *time* validity
  cannot be established, and covers retry-after-restart. It is silent on the case
  where the *replay/request-id ledger itself* is lost, corrupted, or unreadable
  (short of a full owner reset). State the default explicitly: **absent verifiable
  freshness state, privileged requests are refused, not accepted.** Otherwise
  exactly-once has an undefined edge two implementations could resolve oppositely.

- **F-B — digest canonicalization is a shared contract invariant (severity: medium; target ai0.1, consumed by b6j.2).**
  The request binds an "argument digest," but the contract does not state that the
  signer and the verifier MUST compute that digest over the *same canonical form*.
  The concrete encoding rightly belongs to ai0.1; the *"must agree, byte-exact"*
  property belongs to this contract. Pin it so divergent canonicalization cannot
  either break every signature or (worse) let a semantically different argument
  match a digest. (Cf. the byte-exact wire-contract discipline already in
  `portal.rs::Probe::success`.)

- **F-C — concrete device-specific proof + physical ceremony is unselected (severity: high; target bf7.1).**
  The entire authority root rests on "device-specific proof + a fresh physically
  armed ceremony," whose concrete mechanism the contract deliberately does not
  choose (WPS WAN-SSH window explicitly rejected as insufficient). This is honest
  scoping and correctly does not block accepting the contract, but bf7.1 is blocked
  on a real design decision (factory key? hardware attestation/TPM? out-of-band
  code?) and must not smuggle in "unaudited custom key exchange or a trusted
  browser origin," which the design already forbids. Flag before any claiming impl.

- **F-D — issuer-revocation propagation under partition (severity: low; verify ai0.9).**
  Revocation of a *grant* is well specified; revocation of an *issuer* (owner bumps
  authorization state / epoch) rides the same partition bound ("bound stale
  authorization with grant expiry," "show pending enforcement"). This appears
  coherent but should be an explicit ai0.9 test vector, since a partitioned node
  trusting a since-revoked issuer is the highest-impact stale-authority case.

## Material human decision (identify, do not decide here)

- **Maximum offline validity window / partition authority posture.** The design
  bounds stale authorization "with grant expiry" and defers the *number* to ai0.1
  with negative vectors. The **size** of that window is an owner/product policy
  call trading availability (a partitioned house can still operate on unrevoked
  grants) against safety (the window during which a revoked-but-unpropagated grant
  still works). This should be decided by a human and recorded, not settled
  implicitly inside the ai0.1 implementation. The recovery-without-owner-presence
  question is already answered by the design (only via a pre-enrolled recovery
  credential; otherwise a destructive local reset) and needs no new decision.
