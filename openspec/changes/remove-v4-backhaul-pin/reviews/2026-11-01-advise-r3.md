## Blind take

Written before opening the change delta, design, tasks, or prior reviews; based on the proposal, living spec, cited code, and spike evidence.

1. Pin the connection identity to `router_ula(local guild, authenticated endpoint id)`; a packet destination or advertised address is not authentication.
2. Refuse to remove `10.254` until both IPv4 and IPv6 forwarding resolve Babel neighbors through that identity without reversing `fe80::H` into IPv4.
3. Pin overlay neighbor learning to the authenticated connection carrying Hello/IHU, with ambiguity, disconnect, and withdrawal removing stale selections.
4. Require a usable local house `/128` before binding, preserving DAD checks and recorded-address ownership through restart, guild change, and interface recreation.
5. Refuse wildcard fallback and residual preset sockets; the spike explicitly shows that adding one IPv6 bind leaves IPv4 transports alive.
6. Require Babel over physical `br-mesh` to establish peer host routes before any Iroh connection, including overlay mode where the current renderer only lists `mjolnir0`.
7. Refuse outer Iroh recursion into `mjolnir0`, including after an overlay host route wins or the physical route disappears; relays need the same separation.
8. Preserve the complete modified requirement's legacy addressing, house/admitted host filters, projection retries, gateway export, and route-withdrawal behavior.
9. Require a relay-disabled cold-start fixture with real Babel, no static peer routes, no preexisting connection, and subsequent IPv4/IPv6 traffic plus route-loss checks.
10. Tradeoff: strict house-only binding deliberately sacrifices connectivity without a usable guild address; keeping a hidden IPv4 escape would invalidate the replacement.

## Comparison with the current contract

This comparison was written after the blind take and before reading either prior review.

- Blind concerns 1–3 are answered by the local-guild/authenticated-endpoint derivation, explicit Hello/IHU association on the Iroh connection, ambiguity rejection, and separate route-withdrawal versus disconnect behavior. The house-host and admitted-transit examples distinguish destination from peer key; IPv4 keeps its payload and uses the announcing neighbor.
- Concerns 4–5 and 10 are answered by usable-address gating, `clear_ip_transports`, no wildcard fallback, endpoint closure on address/interface changes, and paced recovery. The contract also makes explicit bind an override subject to the startup/lifecycle rules. The spike is correctly limited: it did not clear preset transports or exercise DAD and recovery.
- Concerns 6–7 are answered at the behavioral-contract level: physical Babel starts before any dial even in overlay mode, supplies a `br-mesh` host route, and does not depend on Iroh. Outer traffic must use that route regardless of a competing `mjolnir0` metric; losing it fails closed with relays disabled. Relay traffic also stays off the overlay. Inner host forwarding remains on the overlay. These are distinct, testable obligations, not a claim that binding the source address alone achieves separation.
- Concern 8 is answered by retaining legacy `/64` assignment, recorded-address ownership, usable-house publication, exact host allows, admitted-prefix projection semantics, restart retry, and gateway export. The operator-reference requirement is a separate living block and is intentionally outside this delta, as the packet directs.
- Concern 9 is answered by the isolated cold-start journey and the three scenarios covering Babel-supplied bootstrap, concurrent underlay/overlay host routes, and underlay-route loss. The implementation must actually run those checks; the existing manually routed spike cannot stand in for them.

## Steelman against the blind take

My initial concern could overreach into requiring a particular routing-table, socket-mark, or Babel-process arrangement. The author has instead specified the source of reachability, the startup dependency, and externally observable route-selection invariants. That is enough to constrain implementation without choosing a competing design. In particular, overlay mode no longer assumes its current single-interface Babel renderer can bootstrap its own transport. An endpoint may exist before a peer route, but a direct dial must wait; established outer traffic cannot silently fall into the inner path. The deliberate no-guild/no-endpoint tradeoff and unchanged walked inventory column are disclosed, not hidden recovery paths.

## Prior-review reconciliation

Both prior reviews were read only after the comparison and steelman above were persisted. R1 and R3 remain answered: authenticated neighbor association and endpoint lifecycle are specified. R2's remaining portion, restated as R4 in round 2, is now answered by an explicit physical Babel route source, startup independent of Iroh in both LAN and overlay modes, no static peer route, and source/traffic-specific separation of outer and inner uses of the peer `/128`. The retained Babel-only reachability clause now names both interface roles. The new cold-start scenario starts without a hand-installed peer route or open connection, learns the physical route, then dials and exercises overlay forwarding. Competing-route and path-loss scenarios cover the recursion risk. No additional owed contract box is necessary.

Implementation must demonstrate the specified behavior with actual route selection and traffic, not merely check that the renderer contains `br-mesh`. The contract leaves the mechanism open, but requires outer traffic to retain the physical path even when the overlay route is preferred and to stop when that path is lost with relays disabled. This is acceptance of a testable architecture, not acceptance of an unimplemented routing mechanism.
