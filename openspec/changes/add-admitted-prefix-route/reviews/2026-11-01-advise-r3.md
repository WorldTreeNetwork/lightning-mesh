## Blind take

Written before opening design.md or tasks.md, from Why, the living permit spec, the current ADDED delta, and app_tun.rs.

1. Pin admission to the existing verified bilateral predicate and its trusted authority snapshot.
2. Pin local identity to the guild stored when this TUN session opened; sync must not select another guild.
3. Refuse own-house, unrelated-house, or arbitrary stock prefixes as foreign routes.
4. Pin installation to an up session and a device route, without assigning a foreign interface address.
5. Treat the presented pair set as desired state; an omitted pair must remove the session's route.
6. After successful replace, observation failure must retain ownership and report failure, preserving later deletion.
7. Confirmed absence can release ownership; failed deletion or uncertain absence must preserve retry state.
8. Refuse treating a command's success or an unreadable route table as proof of the desired kernel state.
9. Tradeoff: conservative ownership can prompt redundant cleanup, but forgetting a possibly installed route loses revocation.
10. Require evidence for write-success/read-failure followed by empty-set cleanup; Babel export and overlay demux remain separate work.

## Comparison and steelman

The current design and ADDED delta explicitly answer the remaining round-two concern: a successfully written prefix stays owned when its observation fails, while confirmed absence releases it. The implementation agrees. At `admin/src-tauri/src/app_tun.rs:110`, `Ok(true)` retains the prefix, `Ok(false)` reports failure without retaining it, and `Err(())` both pushes it into `kept` and sets `failed`. The assignment at line 119 preserves cleanup responsibility before returning `Err`. On a later empty pair set, the deletion loop therefore visits that prefix; confirmed absence drops it, while presence or observation failure keeps it retryable and reports failure (line 84). `route_present` distinguishes command execution/status failures from a successful listing with no matching route (line 166).

The regression at `app_tun.rs:540` now exercises the exact counterexample: observation is forced to fail after replace, an independent route listing confirms the foreign route exists, observation is restored, and an empty sync removes it. The additional sequence at line 549 suppresses deletion and fails its observation, then confirms that a subsequent empty sync actually deletes the retained route. The earlier failed-delete regression remains at line 530. These source assertions address the owed transitions; they are not a claim that I executed the privileged test.

The strongest objection to my blind take is that strict admission validation at every layer could expand this small route adapter into another authorization system. The existing boundary answers it: the caller supplies `admitted_pairs` derived from verified directions and a trusted authority snapshot; public struct construction is not itself an authorization boundary. `permit.rs` remains pure. Likewise, confirmed absence after a failed delete is sufficient to release ownership even if the command reported an error: cleanup's required outcome already holds. Conservative ownership is needed for uncertainty, not for proven absence.

The session-bound guild and failed-delete fixes accepted in round two remain intact. Sync has no guild parameter, uses `Session.guild`, excludes its own derived prefix, and ignores unrelated pairs. It writes device routes without assigning the foreign /48 and installs nothing on a down session. Caller-driven pair presentation remains the contract; new watchers, withdrawal operations, Babel export (`mjolnir-mesh-bny6.5`), and overlay demux (`mjolnir-mesh-bny6.6`) are outside this review. The design lists no unverified hypotheses; a spike is optional before act, not owed here.
