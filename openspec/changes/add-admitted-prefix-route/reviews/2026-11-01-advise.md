## Blind take

Written before opening design.md or tasks.md, from the proposal Why, living permit spec, ADDED route delta, and cited code only.

1. Pin the presented, verified two-direction pair as the route prerequisite; do not invent expiry or a withdrawal operation.
2. Pin omission as a local route reconciliation input, not revocation of durable signed evidence.
3. Pin the pair to the house whose app TUN is open; a caller-provided different guild must not silently change that identity.
4. Pin the foreign /48 as a device route and keep the interface address at its app /128.
5. Refuse stock prefixes, unrelated pairs, and this house's own /48, including a derived-prefix collision.
6. Pin the trusted boundary from direction_from_signed through admitted_pairs; public pair structs alone do not prove authorization.
7. Require failed route deletion to remain observable and retryable; forgetting an installed route after a failed delete violates omission semantics.
8. Require repeated sync and session shutdown to converge without deleting routes belonging to other sessions.
9. Refuse claims of end-to-end reachability from route installation alone; router export and overlay demux remain separate work.
10. Tradeoff: explicit caller presentation keeps this node small, but callers must supply a complete current set and drive reconciliation when that set changes.

## Comparison and steelman

The proposal and design correctly keep `admitted_pairs` pure and unchanged. Removing a route follows absence from the caller's presented set; it does not expire or withdraw an op. The device-route command does not assign a /48, the peer /128 remains separate, and the down-session guard prevents installation. Babel export and overlay demux remain outside this node. These answers satisfy the central contract concern.

The strongest case for acceptance is that this is a small caller-driven API over already validated pairs and an already open TUN. Rechecking signatures here or requiring a new UI, log watcher, withdrawal kind, or end-to-end forwarding proof would expand the work without improving this boundary. I accept that scope, including the trusted provenance of `AdmittedPair`; public construction alone is not a separate blocker. A spike is optional, not a prerequisite: `Unverified hypotheses` says none.

Two gaps remain in the cited implementation, and neither the design nor the completed task boxes accounts for them.

## Owed before acceptance

1. **Keep failed removals retryable.** In `admin/src-tauri/src/app_tun.rs:68`, deletion errors are discarded. With an installed prefix P and an empty next set, a failed delete leaves P in the kernel, but line 96 replaces `owned_prefixes` with an empty vector. Every later empty sync skips P, even after the command failure clears. The void return also hides the failure. This contradicts the omission-removes-route requirement; it is more than a transient failure to achieve an OS operation. Preserve pending cleanup until removal is confirmed, distinguish an observation failure from confirmed absence, and expose unsuccessful reconciliation to the caller. Verify a failed deletion followed by a successful retry while the session remains open. Eventual device teardown does not satisfy removal during that session.

2. **Bind “this house” to the open session.** `sync_admitted_routes` trusts a fresh `local_guild` argument at line 59, while `Session` at line 134 retains no guild identity. Concrete counterexample: open a TUN for guild A, then sync with `local_guild = B` and a valid admitted A/B pair. `foreign_prefixes` selects A's prefix and installs the open session's own /48. With a valid B/C pair, the same call routes a pair unrelated to A. No forged permit is required. Derive or validate the local guild against the session's opening identity and explicitly exclude its own /48. A caller convention is plausible, but it is neither stated as a precondition nor enforced, and the current API permits exactly the outcomes the delta forbids. Verify both counterexamples, correct-side selection, and continued rejection of the own prefix.

The existing pure fixture test (`app_tun.rs:351`) only uses a matching local guild and distinct prefixes. The route test (`app_tun.rs:443`) exercises successful deletion and returns immediately when not root. Neither discharges the two findings. The owed tests should exercise these transitions rather than merely assert absence of an unrelated stock literal.
