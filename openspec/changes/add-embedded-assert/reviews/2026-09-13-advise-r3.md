## Blind take

1. Pin `identity.request` to the registered card's exact `WindowProxy` and entry origin, and require exactly one transferred `MessagePort` on that authenticated window event.
2. Treat that port as a requester-selected, one-shot response capability: send one token or terminal error on it, close it, and never also deliver identity material with `window.postMessage`.
3. Refuse missing or multiple ports, reused requests, invalid card state, navigation/removal, expired consent state, and any request received while `hello.mesh` has any parent, including itself.
4. A requester can transfer its peer endpoint to another document before requesting; the host cannot prove who holds it, so the security claim must be capability delivery to the authenticated requester-designated endpoint, not an untransferable document identity.
5. Because a `MessagePort` message has no trustworthy origin, the host must accept no commands, approval, renewal, or audience/nonce data from the response port; only the original authenticated window event establishes authority.
6. Pin audience and nonce from the authenticated request, keep them immutable through consent, and revalidate the card, deadline, and one-shot pending-record ownership immediately before signing.
7. Make the 60-second deadline decisive even against a click already dispatching: atomically claim a still-live record, otherwise emit/close with `interaction_required` and sign nothing.
8. Per-card cooldown may reset only on visitor-authenticated host UI actions; iframe reload, self-close, bridge messages, or any app-driven reopen surrogate must not reset or shorten it.
9. Preserve global serialization only if expiry releases the slot and rejected competing requests neither inherit consent nor extend, replace, or reset the incumbent record or either card's cooldown.
10. Tradeoff: a document-scoped port closes the navigation race cleanly, but it replaces origin-authenticated response delivery with a delegable capability and therefore needs an explicit one-way protocol plus compatibility wording for the accepted bridge envelope.

The ten lines above were persisted before opening the amended delta, `design.md`,
`proposal.md`, `tasks.md`, or the accepted dependency's contract delta.

## Comparison and steelman

The amendment chooses the right primitive for the round-2 navigation race. An
authenticated window message transfers a response capability, the pending record
captures that capability, and success and failure leave only through it. A later
same-origin document inherits the iframe's `WindowProxy`, but it does not
automatically inherit a port endpoint retained by the old document. This closes
the accidental pre-`load` replacement delivery that exact-origin
`window.postMessage` could not close. The unchanged payload builder and verifier
also keep audience, nonce, signature domain and five-minute assertion lifetime
on the already-built v1 protocol rather than inventing a second token.

The bounded-consent shape is likewise defensible: one sheet remains auditable,
the host owns a 60-second upper bound, competing cards cannot overwrite it, and
each unapproved ending lengthens a per-card quiet period. The fail-closed framing
text now treats foreign and same-origin parents alike and orders refusal before
all identity-bearing work. Those are the right answers to round 2. The remaining
issues are not arguments for returning to window delivery or a queue; they are
the missing limits of the chosen capability and lifecycle model.

## Round-2 closure audit

1. **Pre-`load` replacement delivery — closed for involuntary navigation.** The
   request must transfer exactly one port and the pending record captures it
   ([delta lines 5–20](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L5)); tokens are forbidden
   on window delivery ([lines 141–149](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L141)); and
   the named regression puts a message-capable replacement document before the
   parent's `load` ([lines 172–176](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L172)). A new
   document does not acquire the old endpoint merely by occupying the frame.
   The broader claim that the endpoint necessarily remains in the requesting
   document is a new provenance overclaim, addressed below.
2. **Unbounded cross-card denial — closed in its core mechanism, but the edge
   semantics still need pinning.** Host expiry, immediate competing-card failure,
   and 30-second exponential per-card cooldown are normative at
   [lines 17–34](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L17), with slot-release and
   denial-loop scenarios at [lines 66–76](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L66).
   This prevents one untouched request from owning the slot forever. The clock
   boundary and the meaning of visitor "re-open" remain underspecified enough to
   reopen the lever in implementation, as finding 3 explains.
3. **Same-origin framing and stale implementation wording — closed.** The delta
   now refuses *any* parent before identity load, approval reads, listener
   registration or signing ([lines 94–103](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L94));
   the same-origin scenario names both the approved `/assert?prompt=none` and
   embedded-bridge cases ([lines 117–121](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L117));
   and [task 9](../tasks.md#L9) carries that ordering and coverage into the act.

## Send-back findings

1. **The document-provenance claim is stronger than a transferred port can
   provide.** The platform exposes the received endpoint, not proof of which
   document created it or which context retains its peer. A requesting document
   may transfer `port1` to any same- or cross-origin document before it sends
   `port2` to the host; the host will then deliver the token to that delegated
   holder. Therefore “created by the requesting document” at
   [delta lines 9–10](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L9) is unenforceable, the
   design's “only that document holds `port1`” at
   [design lines 40–43](../design.md#L40) is not guaranteed, and the proposal's
   “can't reach any document except the one that asked” at
   [proposal lines 22–25](../proposal.md#L22) is false literally. This does not
   make the design unsafe against a navigated replacement: an app deliberately
   delegating the token it requested could disclose that token after receiving
   it anyway. State the honest boundary instead: the host delivers only to the
   one-shot response capability designated on an authenticated request from the
   registered card; replacement documents do not inherit it absent deliberate
   delegation. Do not claim the host authenticates endpoint ownership.

2. **The port is not yet specified as strictly one-way.** A `message` event on a
   `MessagePort` has no useful sender origin with which to repeat the window
   envelope check. The original window event at
   [delta lines 5–15](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L5) can authenticate the
   request, but nothing says the host must never listen to or interpret later
   app messages on the port. Require the host to accept all app-to-host identity
   data only on that original source-and-origin-checked window event; install no
   command path on the response port; ignore any incoming port message; send at
   most one terminal response and close. Test that port messages cannot change
   audience, nonce, pending ownership, consent, or produce a second response.

3. **Expiry and cooldown reset need event-boundary semantics.** “60 seconds
   pass” ([delta line 22](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L22)) and “still
   pending when Allow runs” ([lines 90–92](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L90))
   do not decide a delayed timer versus a click already dispatched. Store an
   absolute host deadline and check it when any request or decision is handled:
   at/after the deadline expiry wins; before it, Allow atomically claims the
   live record before signing. This also lets B release an overdue A even if A's
   timer callback has not run. Similarly, “visitor re-opens the card from the
   shelf” ([lines 29–34](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L29)) must mean a direct,
   trusted host-UI gesture, not iframe navigation/reload, bridge traffic,
   teardown/recreation, or any app-triggerable surrogate. Name regressions for
   the delayed-timer/click boundary and for failed app-driven reset attempts.

4. **The accepted bridge contract must be amended for compatibility.** Its
   receive rule is compatible with the request: `identity.request` is still an
   outer window message and must retain exact `event.source` plus `event.origin`
   checks and the 16-KiB/serialization envelope rules. Its exhaustive v1 type
   list and send rule are not compatible: the accepted contract currently says
   v1 consists only of `ready`, `init`, `resize`, and `open`, and that hello.mesh
   sends messages only with `targetOrigin` equal to the entry origin
   ([accepted contract lines 155–170](../../add-mini-app-contract/specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L155)).
   A `MessagePort.postMessage` has no `targetOrigin`, while this change adds
   `identity.response`. This change must carry a **MODIFIED Bridge envelope**
   delta: add `identity.request` and port-carried `identity.response` to v1;
   preserve source/origin/size checks for the outer request; preserve exact
   `targetOrigin` for all window-sent host messages; and define the sole identity
   response exception as a one-shot message on the request's transferred port,
   never a window message. The exact owed box belongs on this change, not as an
   edit to the already accepted dependency.

## Disposition

Send back for an honest capability-provenance invariant, an explicitly one-way
port, deterministic expiry/reset semantics, and reconciliation with the accepted
bridge envelope. The original pre-`load` replacement race and same-origin frame
refusal are otherwise closed, and the MessagePort design should remain.
