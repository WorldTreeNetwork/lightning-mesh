## Blind take

1. The task must key pending/cooldown state by stable service name, never by card or mutable endpoint fields.
2. The pending lifecycle must use one absolute deadline and an atomic claim so concurrent handlers cannot both complete it.
3. Response-eligible requests need a closed outcome table with exactly one terminal result.
4. Cooldown must survive card removal, same-name re-creation, and same-name republish.
5. Only a fresh trusted gesture may reset that service name's cooldown.
6. Reopening an already-open app must focus the existing card and preserve its pending request.
7. A pending request may end only through the normative outcome table, not merely through a focus action.
8. Tradeoff: stable name-scoped throttling may delay a legitimately recreated app, but prevents churn from bypassing the security boundary.

The take above was written before opening the amended delta, `tasks.md`, or
`design.md`. It states concerns the amendment had to answer, not a competing
design.

## Comparison and steelman

absolute 60-second host deadline, atomic claim, the single outcome table for
response-eligible requests, and cooldown keyed by service name. It expressly
preserves cooldown across card re-creation and same-name republish and limits
reset to approval or a trusted shelf gesture
([tasks line 11](../tasks.md#L11)). That matches the delta's deadline and claim
ordering ([delta lines 29–39](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L29)), eligible
outcome law ([delta lines 41–63](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L41)), and
service-name cooldown boundary and reset set
([delta lines 75–90](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L75)).

The amended threat row now distinguishes focus from termination: tapping an
already-open app only focuses its sole card and continues its pending request;
request termination is delegated to the normative outcome table
([design line 10](../design.md#L10)). This aligns with the one-card focus rule
([delta lines 294–304](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L294)) and the explicit
focus scenario ([delta lines 330–334](../specs/mesh-mini-apps/spec.md#L330)).

I scanned `tasks.md`, `design.md`, and `proposal.md` once more against the full
delta. Their instructions agree on authenticated window input, one-shot
port-only output, response eligibility, global pending state, deadline/claim
ordering, service-name cooldown, origin-changing republish, trusted focus,
framing refusal, and silent-sharing disclosure. No other behavioral

## Nits (non-blocking)

- The design threat row lists only the card-lifecycle endings after saying
  "per the outcome table." The table reference makes the complete set
  normative, so the abbreviated examples do not change behavior.
- The base lifecycle task says cooldown survives card re-creation but names
  port and IP republish explicitly in its final test examples. The general
  instruction plus the task requiring one test per delta scenario is
  sufficient; this is not a contradictory implementation direction.

## Disposition

and the remaining wording nits do not alter behavior.
